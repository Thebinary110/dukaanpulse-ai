# DukaanPulse AI

**Turn payment signals into profitable merchant actions.**

A hackathon prototype of an AI growth copilot for small Indian merchants. It detects a business opportunity in
payment data, explains the evidence, proposes an offer, asks the merchant for approval, simulates the campaign,
and measures the result against a holdout group.

> **Synthetic data and simulated actions only.** The bakery ("Sunrise Bakery"), its customers and every payment
> are generated by a seeded simulator. No real merchant or Paytm data is used. The app never sends messages and
> never moves money. Built for the Paytm Merchant Growth AI Hackathon; **not affiliated with or endorsed by Paytm.**

## Status

- **Stage 1 (foundation):** done. App shell, design tokens and the tested domain layer.
- **Stage 2 (Command Center):** done. The Overview screen has:
  - a 7/30/90-day range selector, kept in the URL as `?range=`;
  - four metrics compared with the previous equal period;
  - a revenue trend chart with a table view;
  - the detected growth opportunity, with observed evidence and a separately labelled estimate;
  - a recommended next step;
  - sales by time of day and the new vs returning customer mix.
- **Stage 3 (Opportunity Explorer):** done. `/opportunities` shows:
  - an observed summary, with the estimated exposure kept separate;
  - an evidence scatter chart with the detector's own flag line;
  - a sortable, filterable, paginated customer evidence table and the methodology;
  - a four-option comparison with validated, editable assumptions;
  - a ranked recommendation, with "Draft campaign" and "Dismiss opportunity".
  The draft is stored in shared state; Campaign Studio currently shows a summary of it.
- **Stage 4 (Campaign Studio):** done. `/campaigns` loads the draft from shared state and offers:
  - structured offer rules that drive the economics;
  - an editable message with live preview, character count and offer-terms validation;
  - an audience review with disjoint treatment and holdout groups;
  - an economics panel that separates observed, assumed and estimated values;
  - an approval checklist driven by real validation, plus a confirmation dialog;
  - a deterministic, skippable simulated run, and a restart action.
- **Next:** Results analysis (Stage 5). Results currently shows only the headline simulated outcome.

## How the domain works

**Units.** Money is integer paise (`…Paise`); days are 0-based indices in IST from the dataset start; timestamps
are epoch milliseconds; rates are fractions.

**Synthetic data** (`generator.ts`, seed `20261020`). The data covers 180 days (23 Apr to 19 Oct 2026) for a
fictional bakery in Bengaluru, with the analysis date set to 20 Oct 2026:
- A recurring base of 900 customers: regulars at 1.5–4 visits/week, occasional customers at 0.25–0.9/week and
  rare customers.
- About 7 one-time walk-ins per day.
- Weekday and weekend patterns, plus 8% growth across the period.
- About 18,660 payments in total, around 103 orders/day over the last 30 days, AOV of about ₹256 and a 30-day
  repeat-customer share of about 69%.
- Noise: a quarter of regulars take a 7–18 day break mid-period.
- **Planted signal:** 40 established morning regulars stop visiting 21–42 days before the analysis date. A small
  residual chance (3%) of a stray visit remains.

**Detection** (`detectors/lapsedRegulars.ts`). The detector reads only transactions. It never sees which customers
were planted.
- **Rate:** each customer's daily visit probability is estimated from their own history up to their last visit.
  The current gap is excluded so it can't skew the estimate it is tested against.
- **Flag rule:** a customer is flagged when all of these hold:
  - they have at least 6 visit-days over at least 28 days of history;
  - their rate is at least 1 visit/week;
  - their gap is at least 14 days;
  - P(gap this long | usual rate) = (1 − p)^(empty days) is below 0.01.
- **Expected false alarms:** the app also reports how many flags chance alone would produce (regulars tested × 0.01).

**Measured detector performance.** These come from `evaluate.ts` and are asserted in `detector.test.ts`. The
seeds were not used to tune the settings.

| Seed | Planted | Flagged | True pos. | False pos. | Recall | Recall (never returned) | FP rate (tested regulars) | FP rate (all ordinary customers) | Expected false alarms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20261020 (demo) | 40 | 34 | 30 | 4 | 75.0% | 100% (24/24) | 2.4% | 0.18% | 2.1 |
| 1 | 40 | 34 | 32 | 2 | 80.0% | 100% (28/28) | 1.3% | 0.09% | 2.0 |
| 2 | 40 | 35 | 33 | 2 | 82.5% | 100% (27/27) | 1.1% | 0.09% | 2.2 |
| 3 | 40 | 40 | 34 | 6 | 85.0% | 100% (27/27) | 3.5% | 0.27% | 2.1 |
| 42 | 40 | 35 | 32 | 3 | 80.0% | 100% (32/32) | 1.6% | 0.14% | 2.2 |
| **Pooled** | 200 | 178 | 161 | 17 | **80.5%** | **100%** | **2.0%** | **0.15%** | 10.6 |

How to read these results:
- **Misses:** every missed customer made a stray visit after their lapse began. That reset their gap, so
  they don't look absent by this test. That is the method's limit, not a bug: it detects absence, not a reduced
  frequency.
- **False positives:** there were 17, against 10.6 expected by chance. That is about 1.6×, plausibly because
  mid-period breaks lower some customers' estimated rates.

**Overview metrics** (`overview.ts`, `detectors/lapsedSummary.ts`).
- **Comparison periods:** each period is compared with the equally long period immediately before it.
- **Repeat-customer share:** shown only when at least 28 days of earlier history exist. Otherwise everyone in the
  first weeks of data would look new; this is why the 90-day view has no repeat comparison.
- **90-day trend:** plotted as average revenue per day, by week, so the shorter first bucket (90 = 12 × 7 + 6) is
  not understated.
- **Opportunity wording:** the headline and summary are built from detector output. The daypart is named only when
  at least half of the cohort's visits fell in it.

**Economics** (`financials.ts`). The offer cost also counts customers who would have returned anyway.

```
rT  = baseline return rate + offer uplift            (uplift is an explicit, editable assumption)
Revenue with campaign    = N × rT × (ρ·max(B, minSpend) + (1 − ρ)·B + k·B)
Revenue without campaign = N × rB × B × (1 + k)       (k = later full-price visits per returner)
Net incremental profit   = (with − without) × margin − N·rT·ρ·costPerRedemption − N·messageCost
Break-even rT            = (rB·B·(1 + k)·m + c) / ((ρ·Bo + (1 − ρ)·B + k·B)·m − ρ·d)
```

- Net incremental profit = (revenue with campaign − revenue without) × margin − offer cost − message cost
- Break-even return rate is solved in closed form; it is infinite when a redemption costs more than it earns.

All rates and costs in `interventions.ts` are **illustrative assumptions**, not benchmarks or quoted prices:
- margin 55%;
- baseline 14-day return rate 10%;
- redemption rate 85%;
- ₹0.25 per message;
- one later full-price visit per returner;
- assumed uplifts over the baseline:
  - +20 points for ₹30 off ₹200+;
  - +14 points for a free add-on (₹14 ingredient cost);
  - +7 points for double loyalty stamps (₹4.50 per stamp).

**Decision** (`decision.ts`). Every option is evaluated deterministically on the treatment group: the flagged
customers minus a 20% holdout. The ranking policy is documented in the file:
- **Goals:** "Most profit" ranks by expected net incremental profit. "Most customers back" ranks by incremental
  returners among options that at least break even.
- **No good option:** if no offer is expected to profit, "do nothing" is recommended.
- **Close call:** shown when the runner-up is within 15%, or when the recommended option would lose money at half
  the assumed uplift.
- **Inputs:** they are validated (`parseBounded`, `optionProblems`). Invalid values are never committed, and an
  option with inconsistent settings (for example a discount larger than half its minimum spend) is not ranked.

**Campaign Studio** (`campaign.ts`, `message.ts`).
- **Offer rules vs message:** the structured offer is the only input to the economics. The message is copy: it
  must state the discount and minimum order (or the reward), and it must not mention any other ₹ amount or a
  percentage (`validateMessage`). Changing the offer regenerates an unedited message and flags an edited one.
- **Checks:** `checkDraft` combines name, offer rules, message and audience checks. `SUBMIT_FOR_APPROVAL` in the
  state machine re-checks the name, offer rules and message, so the UI can't be bypassed.
- **Approval:** confirming runs `SUBMIT_FOR_APPROVAL` then `APPROVE`. The approval stores a fingerprint of the
  exact draft; `START_EXECUTION` refuses if the draft no longer matches.
- **Simulation seed:** derived from the dataset seed plus that fingerprint (`simulationSeed`), so the same
  approved configuration always gives the same simulated outcome. `simulationFrame` steps through queued →
  delivered → day 1…14 → redemptions → ready; "Skip to the end" jumps to the last frame, and `COMPLETE` stores
  the outcome.
- **Restart demo:** removes only this app's `sessionStorage` key. Saved state that fails validation is discarded,
  and the Studio says so.

**Shared workspace state** (`state/WorkspaceContext.tsx`, `state/persistence.ts`).
- **Contents:** the scenario state machine plus the explorer's settings, goal and selected option.
- **Storage:** kept in React context and mirrored to `sessionStorage`. On load it is validated and discarded if it
  is malformed or belongs to another opportunity.
- **Draft campaign:** `START_DRAFT` (or `UPDATE_DRAFT`) stores a full `CampaignDraft` (offer, message,
  treatment/holdout IDs, assumptions, return rate) for Campaign Studio.
- **Dismiss:** `REJECT` records the reason. From there, the only ways forward are reopening (`RESET` → `REVIEW`)
  or a new draft. The approval gate is unchanged.

**Simulation** (`simulator.ts`). This is a seeded simulation.
- **Split:** customers are split deterministically into treatment and holdout groups.
- **Uncertainty:** each run draws its actual rates within ±30% (offer) and ±20% (baseline) of the assumptions.
- **Measurement:** lift and profit are measured as a real test would measure them: treatment against holdout.
- **Range:** 500 runs give the P10/P50/P90 profit range.
- **Labelling:** every outcome carries `simulated: true`.

**State machine** (`state/scenario.ts`).

`detected → reviewing → drafting → awaiting_approval → approved → executing → measured`

It also supports reject and reset. Execution is refused unless:
- the scenario is in `approved`;
- an approval record exists;
- the draft still matches the approved fingerprint.

Every invalid transition is refused and tested.

## Known limitations

- **Results:** only the headline simulated outcome so far (Stage 5).
- **Simulated outcome vs estimate:** the default configuration's simulated draw returns 4 of 27 against 0 of 7
  held back, below the expected 8.1. This is one deterministic random draw, with ±30% uncertainty on the assumed
  rate. With 7 held-back customers the comparison is noisy, and the UI says so.
- **Approval identity:** approval is recorded as "Sunrise Bakery owner (demo)". There is no authentication.
- **Economics window:** the model covers a single 14-day campaign window. It does not value a won-back
  regular's future months, so net profit figures are small next to the monthly exposure. This is deliberate and
  conservative.
- **Downside check:** "If it works half as well" is a simple sensitivity check, not a calibrated probability.
- **Revenue trend:** daily comparison lines are aligned by position, not by weekday. In the 30-day view a Saturday
  can be compared with a Monday; the tooltip names both dates.
- **Bundle size:** the JavaScript bundle is about 840 kB (250 kB gzipped; mostly Recharts), and Vite warns about chunk size. This doesn't
  matter for a local demo.
- **Detector:** it detects absence only. A customer who drops from 3 visits/week to 1 is not flagged; recall on
  lapsed customers who made a stray visit is low (see above). It does not correct for multiple testing; instead,
  it reports expected chance flags.
- **Dataset:** one merchant, one category and one opportunity type. Customer behaviour is modelled as independent
  daily coin flips: no seasonality beyond weekday and trend, no festival effects, no price elasticity.
- **Simulation:** simulated outcomes reflect the stated assumptions, not evidence about real customer response.
- **Theme and LLM:** dark theme only for now (the token structure supports a light theme). No LLM narration yet; it
  is optional (Stage 7).
- **Tooling:** TypeScript 7.0 (native compiler) is used. If an editor plugin misbehaves, pin `typescript@5.9`.
